Marble vs. Granite: Which Is Right for Your Kitchen?

August 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Both stones look striking in a kitchen, but a countertop earns its keep in ways a showroom slab never has to prove. Before you pick one because it photographs well, it helps to understand how each stone actually behaves once it is cut, sealed, and living under daily use.

Hardness and heat

Granite is an igneous rock, formed under intense heat and pressure, which makes it naturally hard and heat-resistant. You can set a hot pan on granite without much worry, though a trivet is still good practice. Marble is metamorphic and softer, so it scratches and chips more easily under heavy use, and it does not tolerate direct heat as gracefully.

How each reacts to acid

This is the difference that surprises most people. Marble is calcium carbonate, so it reacts with acidic liquids: lemon juice, vinegar, tomato sauce, even some wines. Left on the surface for a few minutes, they can dull the polish and leave a faint etch mark that a normal wipe will not remove. Granite is largely quartz and feldspar, which are far more acid-resistant, so it shrugs off the same spills.

Look and character

Marble's veining is soft, flowing, and unrepeatable. This is the look people usually mean when they call a stone elegant. Granite has a more granular, speckled character, and darker granites in particular hide daily marks well. Neither stone is more premium than the other; it comes down to whether you want a kitchen that shows its history like a well-worn book, or one built to look freshly finished for years.

Sealing and upkeep

Both stones are porous and need sealing. Marble usually needs resealing more often than granite, since it is softer and typically used harder in a kitchen. A sealed surface, wiped promptly after spills, will outlast an unsealed one by years regardless of which stone you choose.

Not sure which to pick? Bring us your countertop measurements and a photo of your kitchen's light. We can show you both stones side by side before you commit to either.

Making the call

As a rough guide, granite suits a kitchen that gets cooked in daily, wants low maintenance, or has young children underfoot. Marble suits a kitchen that is more showpiece than workhorse, or where the room's light and cabinetry are built around that particular veined look. Either way, ask to see the actual slab before it is cut. Photos and small samples rarely capture how a stone reads at full size.

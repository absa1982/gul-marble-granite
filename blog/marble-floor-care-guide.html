How to Keep Marble Floors Looking New

August 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Marble floors are forgiving to install and stunning underfoot, but the same chemistry that gives marble its warmth also makes it sensitive to the wrong cleaning products. Most of the dulling people blame on wear is actually acid etching from an everyday cleaner.

Skip the vinegar and lemon

Marble is calcium carbonate, so anything acidic, vinegar, lemon-based sprays, some all-purpose tile cleaners, will react with the surface and leave a dull, slightly rough patch called etching. It is not dirt, so scrubbing harder only makes it worse. Use a pH-neutral stone cleaner, or plain warm water with a few drops of mild dish soap, and nothing else.

Wipe spills quickly

Tea, coffee, juice, and cooking oil can all mark marble if left to sit, especially on a floor that gets foot traffic dragging the spill around. A prompt wipe with a damp cloth prevents almost all of this. Keep a soft cloth near the kitchen and entryway for exactly this reason.

Protect the entrances

Grit and sand act like sandpaper on a polished marble floor. Mats at every entrance, and a regular sweep or dust-mop before wet cleaning, prevent most of the fine scratching that dulls a floor's shine over the years.

Reseal on a schedule

Sealant wears down gradually with foot traffic and cleaning, not all at once. A simple water-drop test twice a year tells you when it is time: if a drop of water soaks in within a few minutes instead of beading on the surface, the floor is due for resealing.

When to call a professional

Deep etching, cracks, or lippage where tile edges sit unevenly are beyond a home fix. Professional grinding and repolishing can restore a marble floor that looks permanently dulled, often for less than replacing the affected tiles.

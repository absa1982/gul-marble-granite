Buying Marble or Granite Wholesale: What to Ask First

September 2, 2026 · 6 min read

Ordering enough marble or granite for a full house, a commercial site, or a batch of resale units is a different exercise than picking one countertop slab. The questions that matter change, and a few of them can save a project from an expensive mismatch later.

Ask about batch and lot consistency

Natural stone varies slab to slab, and it can vary more between different lots from the same quarry. For a small job this barely matters. For a large floor or a matching set of counters across multiple units, ask whether your order is being cut from the same lot, and see a sample from that specific batch before it is cut, not just a general sample of the stone type.

Understand wastage before you order

Cutting slabs to fit doorways, curves, and odd room shapes always produces offcuts. A wholesale dealer who cuts in-house can give you a realistic wastage estimate for your layout before you buy, so you are not caught short mid-project or paying twice for a top-up order that may not match the original batch.

Clarify what is included

Wholesale rates on the stone itself are only part of the cost. Ask directly whether cutting, edging, and on-site fitting are part of the quote or billed separately, and whether delivery and handling for heavy slabs is included. Getting this in writing before the order avoids surprises when the final bill arrives.

Get samples before committing to volume

A one-square-foot sample under showroom lighting can look different from a full slab in a room with different light and cabinetry. For any order past a single room, ask to see a full or half slab, not just a chip, before confirming the volume.

Planning a site order? Tell us the site size and stone type you are considering, and we will walk you through batch availability and a realistic wastage estimate before you commit.

Work with one dealer for the whole order

Splitting a large order across multiple dealers to chase a slightly better rate on paper often costs more once you account for batch mismatches, inconsistent cutting, and split delivery schedules. A single dealer handling sourcing, cutting, and installation keeps the stone consistent and the accountability in one place.
